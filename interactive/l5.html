What a Stablecoin Promises

Who Issues the Dollars on Chain

    Question for the room On 8 May 2022, the day before the TerraUSD collapse, the supply of USD stablecoins was 185.4 USD bn. What share of it was algorithmic?
    Answer

    About 11%: 11.5%, or 21.3 USD bn. Three weeks later, on 31 May 2022, the algorithmic share was 0.9%, and in October 2026 it is 0.0%; fiat-backed tokens hold 90.9% and crypto-backed tokens 8.1%. TerraUSD was large, but never the largest: on 8 May 2022 Tether held 45.0% and USD Coin 26.1% of the supply.

    Measuring the Peg

    Daily Peg Deviations of Three Stablecoins

      Question for the room With δ = 15 bp, DAI closes beyond ±δ on 5.2% of days, USDT on 3.6% and USDC on 0.7%. The ▶ button raises δ to 100 bp. At δ = 50 bp, which coin has the largest share of closes beyond ±δ?
      Answer

      DAI, with 0.57% of days (12 of 2,122). USDT and USDC are beyond 50 bp on 0.14% of days each (3 days). USDT is the coin most often beyond 15 bp, but its deviations are small and persistent; USDC has the deepest close (−285 bp on 11 March 2023), but only on a few days. How often a coin breaches a threshold depends on the threshold: the ranking at 15 bp does not carry over to 50 bp.

      Tails and Their Frequency

        Question for the room With x = 15 bp, DAI was beyond on 26.6% of days in 2021 and on 0.3% in 2026; USDT on 8.2% and 3.0%. The ▶ button raises x. With x = 25 bp, how many of the three coins have at least one close beyond x in 2026?
        Answer

        None. In 2026 (to 23 October) no close of USDT, USDC or DAI is 25 bp or more from 1 USD; at 20 bp only USDT has any (0.34% of days). The tails are heavy over 2021–2026 as a whole, because of 2021, May 2022 and March 2023; in 2026 the deviations are small. A heavy tail of the full sample does not mean that large deviations occur every year.

        Rolling 1% VaR of Peg Deviations

          Question for the room With a window of 365 days the VaR 1% of USDC stays at 12.0 bp for a year after the SVB weekend. The ▶ button changes the window length W. With W = 90 days, what is the highest VaR 1% of USDC after the SVB weekend?
          Answer

          About 102 bp, on 12 March 2023, and it stays at 50 bp or more for 89 days. With 90 days the 1% quantile lies at position (90 − 1) × 0.01 = 0.89 between the lowest close (−285 bp) and the second lowest (−79.3 bp): −285 + 0.89 × 205.7 = −101.9 bp. With 365 days it lies between the 4th and 5th lowest, which is why one extreme day hardly moves it. A short window reacts strongly to a single day and forgets it after W days; a long window barely sees it.

          The SVB Weekend, Hour by Hour

            Question for the room USDC reached −1,036 bp at 07:00 UTC on Saturday 11 March 2023, 211 bp below its zero-recovery value of −825 bp. For how many hours of the weekend was the hourly close of USDC below the zero-recovery value?
            Answer

            For 8 hours, all on Saturday 11 March between 06:00 and 14:00 UTC (at 08:00 it was −771 bp). From 15:00 UTC on Saturday the price stayed above the value that a total loss of the SVB deposit implies, although it returned above −100 bp only at 22:00 UTC on Sunday 12 March and above −15 bp at 14:00 UTC on Monday 13 March. With a recovery rate R = 0.5 the value is −412 bp, and USDC was below it for 22 hours.

            Automated Market Makers and Price Formation

            Price Response of an AMM Pool to Imbalance

              Question for the room In a StableSwap pool with A = 2000 the price of X is 0.997 when X is 90% of the pool. As the share of X grows, where does the price of X first fall below 0.99?
              Answer

              Between 90% and 95% of the pool: the price is 0.9914 at 94.0% and 0.9898 at 94.5%. With A = 100 it falls below 0.99 already at about 76%, with A = 10 at about 55%; in a constant-product pool any imbalance moves the price (0.99 at a share of 50.3%). A high amplification keeps the price near 1 until the pool is almost one-sided: the price says little about the stress, the composition says much more.

              Early Warning

              Episodes: Mostly Short and Shallow

                Question for the room At δ = 15 bp there are 30 episodes; 15 last one hour and the median duration is 1.5 hours. The ▶ button raises δ. At δ = 25 bp, how many episodes are there, and what is their median duration?
                Answer

                5 episodes, with a median duration of 7 hours; 2 of them last more than a day. A higher threshold keeps fewer, longer and deeper events: the median peak is 33.6 bp, against 16.6 bp at 15 bp. At 50 bp only 2 episodes remain, the FTX collapse (November 2022) and the SVB weekend (March 2023). An episode beyond 25 bp has also crossed 15 bp, but at 15 bp it is counted inside a longer episode that started earlier.

                Worked Example: Computing the Utility

                  Question for the room In the example U = 0.398 − 0.05 × 2.5 = 0.273. The ▶ button raises the false-alert rate Fm from 0 to 10 a month. At what rate of false alerts does U fall to zero?
                  Answer

                  At 7.96 false alerts a month: the mean credit of the four events, 0.398, divided by the cost of 0.05 per false alert a month. 0.273 / 0.05 = 5.46 is the number of false alerts a month that can be added to the 2.5 already counted. The budget of 2 false alerts a month is a constraint on the choice of the alert threshold, not the point where U changes sign.

                  Results: Four Classifier Families

                    Question for the room CatBoost with α = 0.3 has the highest utility of the table, 0.303. For how many of the six values of α does CatBoost have a higher U than the other three families?
                    Answer

                    For 5 of the 6. At α = 0.5 the random forest is higher (0.233 against 0.149). XGBoost and LightGBM never have the highest U; LightGBM has a negative U for five of the six values. Overlapping 95% intervals of CatBoost and the random forest do not change the ranking of the point estimates; they show that the ranking is uncertain.

                    The Recall–False-Alert Trade-off

                      Question for the room On the test period the rule |dt| ≥ 11 bp detects 4 of the 5 events with 3.18 false alerts a month. The ▶ button raises k from 4 to 25 bp. At the smallest k that meets the budget of 2 false alerts a month on the test period, how many events does the rule detect?
                      Answer

                      3 of the 5, at k = 13 bp, with 1.29 false alerts a month and U = 0.340 (Clopper–Pearson 95% interval for the recall [0.15, 0.95]). At k = 12 bp the rule has 2.26 false alerts a month. This k is chosen with hindsight on the test data; the rule chosen on 2024 under the same budget is k = 20 bp, which detects none of the test events. At k = 15 bp, the event threshold itself, the rule detects nothing in time: it fires only once the breach has started.

                      Recent Data: August to October 2026

                      The Last Twelve Weeks

                        Question for the room In October 2026 USDC priced in USDT was at or above 10 bp in 206 of 552 hours, never at 15 bp. With an alert at |dt| ≥ 10 bp and a 24-hour refractory period, how many alert episodes would the rule have issued from 1 August to 23 October?
                        Answer

                        24, about two a week, none of them followed by a breach of 15 bp. The refractory period merges consecutive hours above 10 bp into one episode, so the count is far below the number of hours. With k = 12 bp there are 10 episodes, with k = 14 bp 2. A level rule cannot tell a persistent small discount of USDT from stress; the alarm of the paper also looks at the pool composition and the liquidity curve.
