Can Machine Learning Predict the Direction of Bitcoin?

Overfitting and What the Model Uses (own)

    Question for the room The ▶ button goes through the 40 walk-forward origins. At how many of them is the out-of-sample accuracy of XGBoost, on the next 63 days, above its in-sample accuracy on the training data?
    Answer

    At none of the 40. In-sample, XGBoost fits 74–85% of past days; out of sample its accuracy on a 63-day block ranges from 38% to 67%. The random forest also never does better out of sample; the logit, which fits only 54–58% in sample, does better at 13 of the 40 origins. A flexible model learns the noise of the training days, so its in-sample accuracy says little about new days. Out of sample, XGBoost beats "always up" in 15 of the 40 blocks, and over all 2,487 test days it scores 51.3% against a base rate of 50.6%.

    Trading the Signals (own)

      Question for the room The ▶ button raises the cost per change of position from 0 to 50 basis points. Which strategy loses the most Sharpe ratio?
      Answer

      XGBoost: its Sharpe ratio falls from 0.89 (no cost) to 0.63 (10 bp) and −0.43 (50 bp); it changes position about 125 times a year. The random forest (81 changes a year) falls from 0.97 to 0.19 and the logit (70 a year) from 0.85 to 0.22. Buy-and-hold pays the cost once and stays at 0.59. XGBoost falls below buy-and-hold at about 12 bp, the logit at about 21 bp and the random forest at about 25 bp. The Sharpe ratio before costs does not rank the strategies after costs: turnover does.

      Data Snooping

      810 Trading Rules (own)

        Question for the room The ▶ button raises the number of independent trials N in the deflated Sharpe ratio from 2 to 810. Up to which N is the winner MOM(2) significant at 95% (DSR above 0.95)?
        Answer

        Only for N = 2 (DSR 0.953). The benchmark SR₀, the best annualised Sharpe ratio expected by chance, rises from 0.09 (N = 2) to 0.22 (N = 5), 0.29 (N = 10), 0.46 (N = 100) and 0.58 (N = 810); the DSR falls from 0.95 to 0.92, 0.89, 0.80 and 0.72. The PSR against zero, 0.97, ignores the search. The rules are strongly correlated, so the effective N is below 810, but even 5 independent trials leave the winner below 0.95. Out of sample (2023–2026) MOM(2) has a Sharpe ratio of 0.45 against 0.90 for buy-and-hold.

        Risk Forecasts: Classical, ML and Pretrained

        Breach Rate and Quantile Score Rank Models Differently (own)

          Question for the room The ▶ button extends the evaluation period of the VaR 1% month by month, from January 2024 to October 2026. Which ranking of the six models is the more stable?
          Answer

          The ranking by mean quantile score. From May 2024 on, GARCH-t has the lowest mean QS at every month end; QReg is lowest in the first four months. The model closest to the 1% breach target changes 10 times, among five models (HS, GARCH-t, EWMA-N, QReg, QGBM). With 10.3 breaches expected in 1,027 days, one breach more or less moves a breach rate by 0.1 percentage point, while the QS uses the size of the miss on every day.

          VaR Paths: Machine Learning Can Jump (own)

            Question for the room On 14 January 2024 the VaR 1% of QGBM, the gradient-boosted quantile trees, jumps from 7.3% to 35.4%. The ▶ button moves day by day through January 2024. What is the QGBM VaR 1% on the next day, 15 January?
            Answer

            8.1%: the jump lasts one day. On 14 January the trees received a −7.9% return on 12 January followed by a 52% fall in volume on 13 January, a combination met in training only in crash periods; on 15 January the inputs (return −2.5%, volume −15%) fall in an ordinary leaf again. GARCH-t moves smoothly, from 9.9% (13 January) to 9.4% and 9.2%; a variance recursion cannot jump like this, and a tree forecast has no memory from one day to the next.
