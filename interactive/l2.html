The Shape of Returns

The Normal Distribution Does Not Fit

    Question for the room Each threshold is k of the asset's own standard deviations. With k = 5, which asset has more days beyond the threshold in 2018–2026, Bitcoin or the S&P 500?
    Answer

    The S&P 500: 9 days against 5 for Bitcoin, where the Normal distribution expects about 0.001 and 0.002 days. The threshold is measured in each asset's own standard deviation (1.21% a day for the S&P 500, 3.35% for Bitcoin), so the count compares the shape of the tails, not the size of the moves: the excess kurtosis is 14.9 for the S&P 500 and 14.8 for Bitcoin. In percent Bitcoin moves more: it exceeds 5% on 10.7% of the days, the S&P 500 on 0.6%.

    Volatility and Correlation

    Volatility Through Time

      Question for the room With a 30-day window the median of Bitcoin's rolling volatility is 54% a year. The ▶ button lengthens the window from 7 to 365 days. What happens to the median?
      Answer

      It rises: 47% with 7 days, 54% with 30 days, 58% with 90 days and 66% with 365 days, close to the 64.1% of the whole sample. A short window rarely contains one of the few very large days, so most short-window estimates lie below the long-run volatility and a few lie far above it: the maximum falls from 364% (7 days) to 106% (365 days). The S&P 500 median moves the same way, from 11% to 15%.

      Volatility Clusters

        Question for the room With m = 10 lags the Ljung–Box test rejects for Bitcoin returns: Q(10) = 20.2 against a critical value of 18.3. The ▶ button raises m from 1 to 30. For which m does the test on returns reject at 5%?
        Answer

        Only for m = 10 and 11 and for m = 21 to 29. Q(m) grows with m, but so does the 5% critical value of χ²(m): at m = 12, Q = 20.6 against 21.0; at m = 30, Q = 43.5 against 43.8 (p-value 0.052). For returns the verdict depends on the number of lags, so the evidence of a predictable direction is weak. For squared returns Q(m) runs from 82 (m = 1) to 296 (m = 30), and every m rejects.

        Calm Follows Calm

          Question for the room The ▶ button moves the lag L from 1 to 60 days: the days are sorted by the absolute return L days earlier. At L = 30, how does Bitcoin's mean absolute return after the most turbulent fifth compare with that after the calmest fifth?
          Answer

          Still higher: 2.39% against 2.07% a day, 1.15 times, against 1.49 times at L = 1; at L = 60 the ratio is 1.03. For the S&P 500 it is still 1.39 at L = 30 (1.02% against 0.73%) and 0.91 at L = 60. A turbulent day raises the expected size of moves for weeks: volatility has a long memory, which the GARCH models of Lecture 3 describe with a slowly decaying variance. Turbulence is not followed by unusual calm: the bars do not turn upside down.

          Does Bitcoin Move with Equities?

            Question for the room With 250 common days the highest rolling correlation of Bitcoin and the S&P 500 is 0.57 (February 2023). What is the highest value with a 30-day window?
            Answer

            0.79 (May 2022), and the lowest falls from −0.16 to −0.64. A correlation estimated on 30 days has a large sampling error (about 1/√30 = 0.18 when the true correlation is zero), so short windows give more extreme highs and lows; the 500-day window gives 0.47 and −0.07. The correlation from 2020 onwards, 0.39, does not depend on the window.

            Is the Market Efficient?

            Variance Ratios by Sub-Period

              Question for the room The ▶ button raises the horizon q from 2 to 30 days. Over these 29 horizons and the nine bars, 261 tests at 5%, how many times does a bar cross ±1.96?
              Answer

              10 times, 3.8% of the tests: the S&P 500 in 2018–2021 at q = 2 (Z* = −2.31), and Ethereum in 2016–2017 for q = 22 to 30 (Z* up to 2.48 at q = 30). Bitcoin never crosses. Under a random walk about 5% of 261 tests, 13, would reject by chance, and tests at neighbouring horizons are strongly dependent, so the nine rejections of Ethereum at q = 22–30 are one finding, not nine. Not rejecting at q = 5 does not settle every horizon, but these rejections are no evidence of weekly trends either.

              Efficiency Through Time

                Question for the room With q = 5 no 500-day window rejects VR(5) = 1 for any of the three assets. Switch the horizon to q = 2. Which asset has the largest share of rejecting windows?
                Answer

                The S&P 500: 24 of 120 windows (20%) reject VR(2) = 1, all with Z* below −1.96 (lowest −2.73); Ethereum 2 of 163, Bitcoin none of 185. VR(2) − 1 is close to the lag-1 autocorrelation, and the daily S&P 500 index has a negative one (VR(2) = 0.875 over 2014–2026). Volatility does not make returns predictable: Bitcoin, the most volatile asset, has no rejecting window.

                Bubbles, Crashes and Network Value

                Drawdowns of Bitcoin, Ethereum and the S&P 500

                  Question for the room Bitcoin spent 76% of the days since 2016 more than 10% below its last record. On what share of the days was it more than 50% below its record?
                  Answer

                  34%, about one day in three; Ethereum 52%, the S&P 500 none (its maximum drawdown is −33.9%). The share falls much more slowly than the threshold rises, because Bitcoin's deep drawdowns lasted for long periods (2018–2020 and 2022–2023). Recovering from −50% needs a gain of 100%.

                  Bitcoin 2017: an LPPL Fit

                    Question for the room The estimated tc is 25 December 2017, with R² = 0.982. The ▶ button moves tc from 2 December 2017 to 31 March 2018, with m and ω fixed. How does R² change?
                    Answer

                    Very little: it stays between 0.962 (tc = 31 March 2018) and 0.982 (25 December 2017); it is 0.975 for tc = 2 December and 0.981 for 1 January. A fit this good for every tc over four months cannot date the crash: the window determines the shape of the past rise, not the critical time. The actual peak, 16 December 2017, has no special place in this R² profile.

                    t̂c Window by Window

                      Question for the room The ▶ button adds the eleven weekly windows one by one, from the window ending 1 October to the one ending 10 December 2017. Does the estimated tc move towards the actual peak, 16 December 2017, as the window end approaches it?
                      Answer

                      No. For the first nine windows the estimated tc stays between 1 and 7 January 2018, with m = 0.9 and ω = 13 at the bounds of the search; the window ending 3 December gives the only interior fit (tc = 28 December), and the window ending 10 December puts tc at the window end itself. More data near the peak did not narrow down the date: the estimates are driven by the bounds of the search.

                      Market Value against Metcalfe Value

                        Question for the room The ▶ button moves the exponent b of the network value from 1 to 4.5. For which b is the spread of the log ratio, market value over network value, the smallest?
                        Answer

                        Near b = 3.6: the standard deviation of the log ratio is 1.45 at b = 2 and 1.29 for b between 3.25 and 3.75. With the constant set so that the ratio is 1 on average in logs, minimising this spread is least squares, so the minimum is at the least-squares slope b = 3.59 of the lecture. The choice of b also changes the reading: the last ratio, October 2025, is 6.5 with b = 2 and 3.2 with b = 3.6. The ratio is a diagnostic of the model, not a fair value.
