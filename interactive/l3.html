Why a Crypto Index?

Today's Winners, Bought in 2018

    Question for the room Switch the purchase date to 1 January 2024, with five coins and equal weights. How large is the gap between the ex-post and the ex-ante basket?
    Answer

    Very large: the ex-ante top 5 of 1 January 2024 (BTC, ETH, ADA, DOGE, LTC) returned −3.0% a year to 30 October 2026, the ex-post top 5 (BTC, ETH, ZEC, DOGE, XMR) 129.8% a year. The shorter the period, the more an ex-post list is made of the recent winners: Zcash enters the top 3 only after its rise in 2025–2026. From 1 January 2018 the gap is 17.6% against 7.1% a year; with N = 2 it disappears, because Bitcoin and Ethereum are the top two at both dates.

    Constructing CRIX

    The Decision Curve on Our Data

      Question for the room The ▶ button runs through the 35 quarterly reselections, March 2018 to September 2026. How often does the first rise of the AIC choose only two constituents?
      Answer

      In 9 of 35 quarters; 3 constituents in 13, 4 in 11, 5 and 6 once each (mean 3.2). The curve always falls steeply from k = 1 to k = 2 (adding Ethereum) and is almost flat after that, so the choice between 2, 3 and 4 moves from quarter to quarter. The smoothed AIC rule (six months, polynomial) chooses 3 in 30 of the 35 quarters: smoothing over six months makes the number of constituents more stable.

      Our Replication on Real Data

      The Replica against the Real CRIX

        Question for the room Change the number of constituents: the AIC rules, the TMI, or a fixed top 1 to 7. Which choice tracks the published CRIX best, and by how much?
        Answer

        All choices with 3 to 7 coins are close: tracking error 9.0% (top 5) to 9.4% (top 3) a year, correlation 0.989–0.990; the AIC rules give 9.3%. Bitcoin alone (top 1) has 15.6% and correlation 0.971; the top 2 has 10.0%. Capping the weights below 50% raises the tracking error of the ECRIX replica from 9.3% to 14.5%. Beyond the first two or three coins the remaining gap comes from coins outside our ten-coin universe, not from the rule.

        The Real CRIX, 2017–2026

        CRIX, 2017–2026

          Question for the room Choose the period 1 January 2023 – 31 October 2026. Did the broad index CRIX beat Bitcoin?
          Answer

          No: CAGR 40.4% for CRIX against 49.3% for Bitcoin, with volatility 47.4% against 46.1% and maximum drawdowns of −56.1% against −53.1%. Over the official history (16 March 2018 – 31 October 2026) the picture is the same: 24.0% against 29.4% a year. In this period Bitcoin's share of the market rose, so a market-value index of many coins grew more slowly and was not less volatile.

          Drawdowns of CRIX and Bitcoin

            Question for the room At 50%, CRIX spent 48.8% of the days more than 50% below its record, Bitcoin 39.9%. What are the shares at 70%?
            Answer

            17.5% of the days for CRIX and 9.5% for Bitcoin. CRIX has two falls deeper than 70%: 2018 (−87.1%, record again on 31 December 2020) and 2021–2022 (−77.4%, record again on 11 November 2024). The broad index stays deeper under water for longer than Bitcoin, because the smaller coins fall more in crypto winters.

            VCRIX: A Volatility Index

            VCRIX, 2018–2026

              Question for the room The ▶ button changes the window of the realised volatility from 7 to 90 days. For which window does VCRIX correlate most with the realised volatility of the previous day?
              Answer

              At exactly 30 days: correlation 0.943 (21 days 0.873, 45 days 0.839, 90 days 0.657); a scan of every window from 5 to 120 days peaks at 30. This is consistent with the construction of VCRIX on a 30-day realised volatility (Kim, Trimborn and Härdle, 2021). With the same-day volatility the correlation is lower, 0.930: VCRIX on day t reflects the returns up to day t − 1.

              Volatility after Falls and Rises

                Question for the room Raise the threshold of a large move to 10%. What happens to VCRIX the day after a fall of more than 10%, and after a rise of more than 10%?
                Answer

                After the 35 falls of more than 10% the log of VCRIX rises by 21.6% on average, after the 19 rises of more than 10% by 18.3%; on the other days it falls slightly (−0.4%). Large moves in either direction raise the volatility index; the effect after falls is somewhat larger, as the leverage effect suggests, but rises also raise it, because VCRIX is built from squared returns.

                VCRIX against GARCH and Realised Volatility

                  Question for the room The ▶ button shifts the GARCH and realised volatility series against VCRIX by k = −10 to +10 days. At which shift is the correlation of VCRIX with the realised volatility highest?
                  Answer

                  At k = −1: 0.931, against 0.916 at k = 0, i.e. VCRIX on day t matches the 30-day realised volatility of day t − 1. For the GARCH one-day-ahead volatility the maximum is at k = 0 (0.844), since it is already a forecast made on day t − 1. For positive k the correlations fall (GARCH 0.53 at k = +10): VCRIX does not anticipate future volatility better than the two models.

                  From Index to Investible Product

                  A Spot Bitcoin Fund against Bitcoin

                    Question for the room Move the start of the period to July 2025 and switch the benchmark clock. What happens to the tracking error and to the tracking difference?
                    Answer

                    With Bitcoin at 16:00 New York the tracking error falls to 1.7% a year (from 3.5% since January 2024), with Bitcoin at 24:00 UTC it is still 16.1%: most of the latter is the time gap between the two prices, not the fund. The tracking difference with synchronous prices stays near −0.26% a year, close to the sponsor fee of 0.25%; with midnight prices it is a noisy −0.06%.
